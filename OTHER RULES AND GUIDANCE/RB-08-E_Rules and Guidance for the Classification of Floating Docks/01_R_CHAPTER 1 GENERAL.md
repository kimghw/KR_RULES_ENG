# Rules and Guidance for the Classification of Floating Docks

> OTHER RULES AND GUIDANCE / RB-08-E / 2025 / EN / Rules

## CHAPTER 1 GENERAL

### Section 1 General

#### 101. Application

- **1.** This Rules applies to survey and construction of floating docks which intend to be registered or which registered with classification.
- **2.** The Rules are made for on the conditions that floating docks will be properly loaded and handled in sheltered waters; they do not provide for special distributions or concentrations of loading. The Society may also require additional strengthening to be fitted in any floating dock which in their opinion, may be subjected to severe stresses due to particular features in her design or when it is desired to make provision for exceptional loaded or ballasted conditions. In these cases particulars and relevant data are to be submitted for consideration.
- **3.** The relevant parts of the **Rules for the Classification of Steel Ships** (hereinafter referred to as「the **Rules**」) apply to essential constructions, machinery and equipment not specified in these Rules, as may be required.

#### 102. Equivalency

The equivalence of alternative and novel features which deviate from or are not directly applicable to the Rules is to be in accordance with **Pt 1, Ch 1** of the **Rules for the Classification of Steel Ships**. *(2020)*

#### 103. Other regulations

These Rules specify the requirements for the Classification of floating docks. Therefore, the attention of owners, builders, and designers is directed to the regulations of national or local governments, or other organizations which may contain safety, health, or other standards applicable to the floating dock.

#### 104. Towage certificate

Where the Society's towage certificate is requested by a builder/owner to enable a dock to be towed at sea special consideration may be required to be given to the strength, freeboard and stability, and other items as considered necessary.

#### 105. Cranes

When the assignment of safe working load of cranes is requested by the builders or the owners, the Society will assign the safe working load in accordance with **Pt 9, Ch 2** of the **Rules for the Classification of Steel Ships**.


### Section 2 Classification Registry

#### 201. Character of classification

- **1.** The class notations assigned to the floating dock classed with the Society are to be in accordance with the requirements specified in **Pt 1, Ch 1, 201.** of the **Rules**. However, the notation "Floating Structure" shall be assigned as a ship type notation.
- **2.** For barge equipped with skid (launching) arrangements, the notation "Launching Skid Barge" shall be assigned as a ship type notation.


### Section 3 Definition

#### 301. Length

The length ($L$) is the distance, in meters, measured on water line when supporting a ship whose displacement is the lifting capacity between the aft end and the fore end of the bulkheads of the floating structures of the dock.

#### 302. Breadth

The breadth ($B$) is the moulded breadth in meters measured at the greatest horizontal distance between the inner surfaces of the outer side plating of wing walls.

#### 303. Depth

The Depth ($D$) is the vertical distance in meters measured at the centerline from the inner surface of the bottom plating to the inner surface of the top deck plating.

#### 304. Safety deck

The safety deck is a watertight deck extending over the length of the wing walls and located below the top deck.

#### 305. Top deck

The top deck is the deck extending over the length of the wing walls to form the top of the wing walls.

#### 306. Pontoon

The pontoon is the structure that extends between and under the wing walls to form the bottom of the dock.

#### 307. Rest ballast water

The rest ballast water is ballast water which can not be discharged by pumps from ballast compartments.

#### 308. Compensating ballast water

The compensating ballast water is ballast water for reduction of stresses and deflections in the dock structures and for adjustment of the trim and heel of the dock.

#### 309. Lifting capacity

The lifting capacity ($Q$) is the displacement of the heaviest ship it is intended that the dock shall lift in normal service.
