# Guidance for DC Distribution Systems

> OTHER RULES AND GUIDANCE / GC-29-E / 2025 / EN / Guidance

## CHAPTER 5 RISK ASSESSMENT

### Section 1 General

#### 101. General

- **1.** The purpose of the risk assessment is to ensure the safety of the ship/human life and the risk to the performance of the ship due to the use of the DC distribution system. Hazards due to reasonably predictable failure within the ship's life cycle shall be considered. However, in the case of small ships, the risk assessment may be excluded in accordance with the shipowner and classification consultation.
- **2.** Risk shall be acceptable and analyzed using recognized risk analysis techniques. At a minimum, loss of function, component damage, fire, explosion and electrical shock shall be considered. This analysis shall ensure that hazards are eliminated wherever possible. The risks that can not be eliminated shall be mitigated as necessary. Details of risks and means to mitigate risks shall be included in the operating manual.
- **3.** Risk assessment shall be carried out in accordance with a recognized standard(eg. ISO 31010) and **Guidance for Approval of Risk-based Ship Design** by qualified personnel with sufficient qualifications.
- **4.** Risk assessment determines the required level, taking into account the needs of the operator and all persons performing maintenance, including but not limited to:
  - **(1)** Location and maintainability of monitoring equipment
  - **(2)** Level of monitoring required (eg. the frequency, type and display of information)
  - **(3)** Who the system is to alert and how they are to be alerted
- **5.** The risk assessment shall include at a minimum, but not limited to:
  - **(1)** All normal and foreseeable abnormal operating conditions
  - **(2)** Equipment layout, arrangement, and location
  - **(3)** Mechanical and electrical failure (e.g., short circuit, earth fault, fire, flooding, cooling failure, and operation exceeding the designed operating parameters)
  - **(4)** Software development and integrity
  - **(5)** Electrical power system protection philosophy (e.g., failure of power control, failure of main power supply, failure of power supply of DC bus, failure of DC breaker or protective device)
  - **(6)** Indicators and alerts
  - **(7)** Control component failure (e.g., temperature/pressure sensor failure, converter/inverter failure, DC bus-tie failure, loss of communication)
  - **(8)** Electrical shock precautions (e.g., measure that personnel cannot access energized equipment is to be given to the system design and equipment layout)
  - **(9)** Risk Assessment Report for Battery System (if applicable) (see the Guidance for Battery Systems on Board of Ships)


### Section 2 Risk Assessment

#### 201. Asset Identification and Considerations

- **1.** In the first step of the risk assessment, all information necessary for a clear understanding of the risk assessment shall be collected and all the requirements for the risk assessment work, such as the purpose of the risk assessment, the scope and the risk assessment level, shall be defined.
- **2.** At a minimum, the following items shall be prepared for risk assessment.
  - **(1)** Diagram of DC distribution system
  - **(2)** Equipment and system information (hardware/software list, system interface, function, operation method)
  - **(3)** Risk acceptance criteria
- **3.** The schematic diagram of the DC distribution system shall be prepared so that it can be easily understood, and the system shall be systematically reviewed.
- **4.** The risk acceptance criterion is a measure to compare the safety target level with the risk level as a result of the assessment, and care shall be taken in setting the risk acceptance criterion. To establish the risk tolerance criteria, various data such as existing accident data and the risk acceptance criterion used in industry shall be considered.

#### 202. Vulnerability Identification

- **1.** Vulnerability Identification is the task of identifying risks and their impacts and mitigation options, and shall be able to identify the different scenarios of the risks to the DC distribution system.
- **2.** Vulnerability identification shall identify the risk factors of the system, the effects thereof, the mitigation measures that have already been applied and the additional applicable measures and list all possible incident scenarios.

#### 203. Risk analysis

- **1.** Risk analysis is the task of expressing qualitatively or quantitatively the risk of identified risk scenarios and shall review the risks and vulnerabilities of DC distribution systems.
- **2.** As a qualitative risk analysis method, a risk matrix may be used that expresses the occurrence frequency and the effect of the risk as a risk index. If sufficient data is available, quantitative analysis can be performed using a widely-used proven risk analysis technique, or the company's own risk analysis method can be applied.

#### 204. Identification of risk reduction measures

- **1.** If a risk scenarios are identified and the currently applied risk mitigation measures for each scenario are identified, a new measure shall be proposed by reviewing the need for risk reduction measures.
- **2.** The risk reduction measures shall be divided into measures to reduce the frequency of risks and measures to minimize the effects of risks.
- **3.** For each identified scenario, scenarios in which the risk exceeds the risk acceptance criterion or is higher than the target risk level shall provide appropriate mitigation measures to reduce the associated risk level.
- **4.** When proposing new risk reduction measures, the costs to be applied to the risk reduction measures and the effects obtained by applying them shall be considered.
- **5.** For all proposed risk reduction measures, the effects to be achieved and possible adverse effects should be evaluated and the treatment of remaining risks after the application shall be considered.
- **6.** Means shall be provided to monitor and record system faults such as voltage surges, voltage drop, common mode noise, high frequency noise, blackout and other conditions that can cause other hazards.

#### 205. Risk Assessment Results

The results of the risk assessment shall be appropriately documented so that the risk level of the DC distribution system, targets that need to reduce risk, and the additional tasks required to reduce of the risk can be easily found. ![](images/image3.png)
